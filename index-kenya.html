These are screening-level, Tier‑1‑style default estimates for early planning and farmer communication — not a certified carbon-credit quantification. Actual sequestration depends on soil type, baseline SOC, rainfall, and management consistency, and typically carries ±30–40% uncertainty at this level. For credit issuance (e.g. Verra, Gold Standard, or Kenya's ASAL carbon projects) you will need field soil sampling and a registered methodology.

Methodology & default factors
  • Soil carbon: practice-level default sequestration rates (tC/ha/yr), converted to CO₂e at 3.667, area-weighted across selected practices. Terracing uses an avoided-erosion default rather than a direct build-up rate. Biochar uses a stable-carbon fraction applied to the input rate.
  • Avoided fertiliser-production emissions: reduction in synthetic N/DAP applied (baseline N × organic substitution share) × a blended manufacture-and-transport emission factor (2.8 kgCO₂e/kg N).
  • Avoided soil N₂O: IPCC-style combined direct + indirect emission factor (1.5% of N as N₂O‑N) applied to the avoided N, converted to N₂O and then to CO₂e at a 100‑yr GWP of 265.
  • Livestock: indicative enteric methane defaults for East Africa — ~35 kg CH₄/head/yr (indigenous zebu, mixed feed) or ~55 kg CH₄/head/yr (zero-grazing dairy cross), GWP 27, shown as context only.
  • All factors are editable in the page source (search "FACTORS") if you have KALRO, county agriculture office, or local soil-test data to substitute.
